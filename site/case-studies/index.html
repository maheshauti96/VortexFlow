Case studies

Five desks. One gesture.

How VortexFlow fits into a day: on call at 3 a.m., in a design review, on a sixty-tab support queue, inside a thesis, and in a hand that cannot afford Cmd-Tab. Each one ends with the setup, and with what it did not fix.

Engineering · On call

Three in the morning, one dashboard.

Paged at 3 a.m., eleven windows from the evening, one Grafana tab that matters. A thumb button and three letters.
Design · Product

Forty artboards and a stylus.

Four Figma windows, one icon. The card shows the file name; the review goes to the TV without hunting the green button.
Support · Customer success

Sixty tickets and a customer's name.

Sixty tabs, on purpose. The right one has a customer's name in the title, and the private window is badged so it never gets mixed up.
Research · Writing

Chapter four is on the other desk.

One Space per chapter, forty PDFs with one icon. Hold to bounce between draft and source; type a word from the title for the rest.
Accessibility · Ergonomics

A thumb instead of a chord.

Cmd-Tab is a chord. A few hundred a day is a problem for a hand that already hurts. One button under the thumb is not.

These studies are composites. The desks, windows and settings are real patterns from real use; the people are stand-ins, so nobody's ticket queue or thesis ends up on a marketing page. Recognize your own desk? Tell us and we will write it up properly, with your permission.
