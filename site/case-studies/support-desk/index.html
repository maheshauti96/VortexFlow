Sixty tickets and a customer's name.

A support lead keeps sixty tabs open on purpose. The one she needs has a customer's name in its title, and the private window she is reproducing a bug in must never be confused with her own account.

7 September 2026

The desk

Aisha runs a five-person support team for a scheduling product. Her Chrome has, at any moment, thirty Zendesk tickets, a dozen Intercom conversations, the macros doc in Notion, the status page, the admin console, and a private window signed into a customer's test account to reproduce whatever they reported. Slack. A Meet call that has been going for forty minutes. A second Chrome window for her own life, which she would like to keep separate.

The problem

Sixty tabs are not a mistake; they are the day. The mistake was finding one. Zendesk tabs all show the same favicon. Scanning the tab strip for a customer's name meant hovering, reading tooltips, and losing the place in the conversation she was typing. The private window and the normal window are both Chrome, and twice she had typed into the wrong one.

What changed

Tickets have the customer's name in the title, so the spiral finds them by name: okon narrows sixty tabs to the two from Okonkwo Logistics, most recent first. zen matches the host and lists every Zendesk tab in the order she touched them, which is the order she wants. The macros doc is a pin, one card away even on the weeks she forgets to open it.

The private window shows Chrome's own icon with a private badge; VortexFlow never fetches a site icon for it, so it never looks like the customer's real account. The Meet tab carries an audio cue while the call is live, so when she needs to unmute she picks the card that is visibly the one making sound. Between tickets she holds the button to bounce back to the conversation she was mid-sentence in.

She granted Screen Recording so Grid could show previews. On a support laptop with nothing confidential on screen that is not already in the ticket, she decided the previews were worth it. Her teammate on the same product declined it and uses the spiral with icons. Both are the same app.

The setup

Trigger
Middle click, knowingly: she opens links in new tabs with ⌘-click instead. Also ⌃⌥Space when she is typing a reply.
Layout
Grid with previews for tickets. Screen Recording granted for this machine only.
Pins
Macros (Notion link) · Status page · Admin console
Cues
Private-window badge on the reproduction window; audio cue on the live call.

What it did not fix

Ticket volume. Nothing about the spiral makes a customer less upset. What went away is the hovering, the tooltips, and the second time she nearly replied from the wrong account.

A composite: the desk, the windows and the setup are drawn from how people actually use VortexFlow, but the person is a stand-in and nothing here is a testimonial. If this is your desk, tell us what we got wrong.
